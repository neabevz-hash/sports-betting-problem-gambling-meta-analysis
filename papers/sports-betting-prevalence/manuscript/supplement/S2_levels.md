**Supplementary File S2.** Instruments, thresholds and case-definition levels (fixed in protocol §13 and completed before extraction, clarification C1)

| Instrument | Full name | Range | L1 | L2 | L3 | Brief screen or L4 | Source of the thresholds |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| PGSI | Problem Gambling Severity Index (9 items of the CPGI) | 0-27 | >=1 | >=3 | >=8 | — | Categories 0 non-problem, 1-2 low risk, 3-7 moderate risk, 8+ problem gambling (Ferris & Wynne 2001); validity of the categories and revised thresholds 1-4 / 5-7 / 8+ (Currie et al. 2013, 10.1007/s10899-012-9300-6); Revised thresholds used in sensitivity analysis 4. CPGI = PGSI for scoring. CPGI, its French version ICJE (Indice canadien du jeu excessif) and versions with items reworded for sports betting score as the PGSI if the item scoring (0-3, total 0-27) is unchanged; other scoring (e.g., the Turkish PSBSI, items 1-3, total 9-27) = adhoc |
| SOGS | South Oaks Gambling Screen | 0-20 | >=1 | >=3 | >=5 | — | Lesieur & Blume 1987: 5+ probable pathological gambler; 3-4 commonly labelled problem or at-risk; Lifetime or past-year versions: time frame coded |
| SOGS-RA | South Oaks Gambling Screen - Revised for Adolescents | 0-12 | >=1 | >=2 | >=4 | — | Winters, Stinchfield & Fulkerson 1993: 0-1 no problem, 2-3 at risk, 4+ problem gambling; Adolescent samples |
| DSM-IV-MR-J | DSM-IV Multiple Response - Juvenile | 0-9 | >=1 | >=2 | >=4 | — | Fisher 2000: 4+ problem gambling; 2-3 at risk; Adolescent samples |
| NODS | NORC DSM-IV Screen for Gambling Problems | 0-10 | >=1 | >=3 | >=5 | — | 1-2 at risk, 3-4 problem, 5+ pathological (Gerstein et al. 1999); psychometrics: Hodgins 2004, 10.1016/j.addbeh.2004.03.017; NODS-SA (self-administered past-year version) is scored the same |
| NODS-CLiP | NODS Control, Lying and Preoccupation items | 0-3 | — | — | — | >=1 (L3-screen) | Toce-Gerstein, Gerstein & Volberg 2009: rapid screen, any positive item; Brief screen: sensitivity analysis 3 |
| DSM-IV-screen | DSM-IV criteria for pathological gambling, self-report (e.g., Stinchfield measure) | 0-10 | >=1 | >=3 | >=5 | — | DSM-IV: 5+ pathological gambling; 3-4 commonly labelled problem gambling; Stinchfield 2003 validation |
| DSM-5-screen | DSM-5 criteria for gambling disorder, self-report | 0-9 | >=1 | >=2 (provisional) | >=4 | — | DSM-5: 4-5 mild, 6-7 moderate, 8-9 severe gambling disorder; threshold lowered from 5 to 4 (Petry et al. 2013, 10.1111/j.1360-0443.2012.04087.x); L2 for DSM-5 screens stays provisional: no included report gave a DSM-5 category below 4 at full-text screening; an estimate below 4 is coded L2 with the note "DSM-5 L2 provisional" |

Yellow light is the instrument's at-risk category (L2); green light includes a score of 1, so L1 is not defined |
| interview | Clinical or structured diagnostic interview (SCID, DIGS, CIDI, MINI and others) with DSM or ICD criteria | NA | — | — | — | L4 | Diagnosis by explicit criteria |

Levels are cumulative: a sample reporting PGSI categories contributes L1 (≥ 1), L2 (≥ 3) and L3 (≥ 8). Categories were extracted as printed and cumulative levels were built at analysis from contiguous categories (`analysis/build_levels.py`).
