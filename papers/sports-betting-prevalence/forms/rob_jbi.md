# Risk of bias: JBI checklist for prevalence studies, decision rules for sports bettors (draft for v1.0)

Source: Munn et al. (2014, https://doi.org/10.15171/ijhpm.2014.71; 2015, https://doi.org/10.1097/XEB.0000000000000054). Each item: **yes / no / unclear / NA**. The AI assistant drafts each rating with a supporting quote and its location; the draft is stored unchanged. R1 verifies every item against the paper and decides (protocol §14). No total score. Items 2, 6 and 9 feed the sensitivity analyses. Adapted from the misophonia rules (`papers/misophonia-prevalence/forms/rob_jbi.md`).

The target population is the population of sports bettors the study claims to describe: sports bettors of a country or state, customers of an operator, university students who bet, school pupils who bet. For bettor subsamples of population surveys, items 1, 2 and 9 are rated on the parent survey.

| # | JBI question | Yes | No | Unclear |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Was the sample frame appropriate to address the target population? | The frame covers the target population: population register or address list of the parent survey; all customers of the operators named; all enrolled students | The frame covers only part of it: one operator's customers for "sports bettors in the country", social-media followers of tipster pages, one class for "students" | Frame not described |
| 2 | Were study participants sampled in an appropriate way? | Probability sampling or census of the frame (for subsamples: of the parent survey) | Convenience, snowball, volunteer opt-in, online non-probability panel (including quota-"representative" panels) | Method not described |
| 3 | Was the sample size adequate? | n of sports bettors ≥ 385, i.e., ±3 percentage points at an expected 10% | Below 385 | — |
| 4 | Were the subjects and the setting described in detail? | Age, sex, setting, country and dates of data collection reported | Two or more of these missing | One missing |
| 5 | Was the data analysis conducted with sufficient coverage of the identified sample? | Missing problem-gambling data ≤ 10% of the sports bettors, or handled | > 10% missing, not handled | Not reported |
| 6 | Were valid methods used for the identification of the condition? | Diagnostic interview (L4), or a validated instrument (PGSI, SOGS, SOGS-RA, NODS, DSM-IV-MR-J, DSM-based screens, PPGM) with its standard threshold, in a language version with published validation | Ad hoc items, study-specific thresholds, brief screens used as a diagnosis (BBGS, Lie/Bet, NODS-CLiP), or versions without any validation in that language | Version or threshold unclear |
| 7 | Was the condition measured in a standard, reliable way for all participants? | Same instrument, time frame, mode and threshold for everyone; reliability reported in the sample or the source | Different instruments or modes across participants without a check | Not reported |
| 8 | Was there appropriate statistical analysis? | Numerator and denominator of sports bettors clear; CI given or calculable; weights used if the design requires them | Percentages that cannot be traced to a numerator and a denominator of sports bettors | — |
| 9 | Was the response rate adequate, and if not, was the low response rate managed appropriately? | Response ≥ 60%, or comparison of responders and non-responders, or weights for non-response | Response < 60% without handling; open online surveys with no denominator of invited people | Not reported |

Additional coding, not part of the JBI rating: `gambling_disclosed` (codebook) — whether the invitation named gambling or betting, which risks self-selection of heavier gamblers; `industry_funding`.
